## Cadence: 2 posts a day

| Slot | Account | Time | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| AM | **Founder profile** | 08:15 ET / 17:15 PKT | US decision-makers checking the feed first thing. Personal posts reach more people than company posts. |
| PM | **Company page** | 12:15 ET / 21:15 PKT | US lunch scroll. After it goes live, reshare it from the founder profile with one line of your own. |

Weekend posts get less reach. Keep them lighter (pop culture, behind the scenes).

## Weekly mix (14 posts)

- **~50% pop-culture lessons**: a sitcom or film moment turned into one CX or ops lesson
- **~20% case studies / proof**: a client journey told as a story, with numbers
- **~20% frameworks / checklists**: saveable, practical content (these get saved and shared)
- **~10% offer**: a soft CTA ("taking on a few brands this quarter, pilot first")

## The pop-culture formula

1. **Hook (line 1)**: the famous line or moment. It has to make sense in the first ~140 characters, before "…see more".
2. **Bridge**: why that scene is exactly what happens in a support or ops team.
3. **Lesson**: 3–5 concrete, practical points using → arrows or numbers.
4. **Proof**: one real number from our work.
5. **Question**: an easy-to-answer, slightly playful question so people comment.
6. **3–5 hashtags**: 2 broad (#CustomerExperience, #Ecommerce), 1–2 segment tags (#Logistics, #Robotics, #DTC), 1 pop-culture tag (#TheOffice).
7. **Link in the first comment**, never in the post body.

### Reference bank

- **Classic sitcoms**: Friends (PIVOT!, "We were on a break!", Joey doesn't share food, Chandler's sarcasm), The Office (Dwight's "False.", "That's what she said" (avoid on LinkedIn), Michael's bankruptcy, Jim's pranks), Seinfeld (Soup Nazi, "serenity now"), Parks and Rec (Ron Swanson's no-nonsense style, Leslie's binders), Brooklyn Nine-Nine, HIMYM ("Suit up", "Legen—wait for it—dary"), Breaking Bad (process, "No half measures", "I am the one who knocks"), Better Call Saul.
- **Current (Sep–Oct 2026)**: *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* (now the top-grossing film ever at the US box office), *Lanterns* on HBO (Sundays through Oct 4, finale ep = good hook), *The Bear*, *Severance*. Check the week's box office and streaming top-10s on Monday and swap in whatever's trending.

### Images: guardrails

- Every card is **original design**: typography, shapes and brand colours. **Never** use film stills, screenshots, posters,
  logos or actors' faces. They're copyrighted, and LinkedIn takes them down when reported.
  Quoting a short famous line as commentary is fine. Reproducing the imagery isn't.
- Don't imply endorsement. We never say "Marvel says…" or suggest a partnership.
- Keep it clean: avoid the edgier sitcom lines (e.g. "that's what she said") and anything political or medical.
- The number on a card must match the portfolio exactly, including qualifiers.

## Growing the company page

- **Invite your connections to follow the page.** LinkedIn gives company pages a monthly allowance of follow invites. Use all of them, starting with ops and CX people.
- Ask the team (every agent and lead) to set OptiFlowCX as their current employer. Each profile links back to the page.
- Reshare every company post from the founder profile with a one-line personal take. Don't just repost it.
- Pin the best-performing case study to the top of the page.
- Once a month, turn the best 3 posts into a newsletter edition on the company page ("The CX Ops Brief").
